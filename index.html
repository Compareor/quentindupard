Act 01

AI can replace the execution.
Not the ideas.

Shipping used to be the hard part. It isn't any more, and everybody noticed at roughly the same time. What's left is the stuff no model will do for you: deciding what's worth building, who you're building it for, how you explain it, and what to charge.

Those are creative calls. Frameworks don't produce them. They come from having sat in the product review and then on the sales call two hours later, and noticing the two rooms believe completely different things about the same customer. That gap is where most of my useful ideas come from, and it's what I'm actually selling.

Act 02

Five places the money
is usually hiding.

01

Positioning & messaging

Cheapest thing to change, usually the biggest lift. Read your homepage and ask whether it could describe three other businesses. If it could, more paid spend just buys you confusion at a higher velocity. This is a writing problem, not a workshop problem.

02

Pricing & packaging

The fastest revenue lever there is, and the one teams touch least because changing it feels dangerous. It usually isn't. Most pricing got set once, early, by two or three people who were guessing. Then nobody touched it again.

03

Activation

Signups are vanity until someone reaches the moment the product actually works. I find that moment, measure how many people get there, and cut everything standing between the two.

04

Expansion revenue

Retention gets all the attention; expansion pays the bills. If your customers can't spend more with you as they grow, you've built a business that has to win new logos forever just to stand still.

05

Product strategy

What to build, and the harder one, what to kill. Most roadmaps are just a record of things nobody was willing to cancel. I'm comfortable being the person who says that out loud.

    Act 08

    Questions I get asked.

    What does a fractional product and marketing operator do?

    Part-time senior product and marketing work for businesses that need the judgement but not a full-time hire. In practice that means pricing and packaging decisions, positioning and messaging, improving conversion and activation, and deciding what to build versus what to kill. Where the answer is something that does not exist yet, I build it: whole products, ecommerce, newsletters, operations automation, and the marketing strategy around them. Most common in B2B SaaS, but the same levers apply to any business with a funnel and a price.

    Do you only advise, or do you build things too?

    Both, and the building is not a side line. I have shipped whole products end to end: ecommerce, newsletters and content engines, internal tooling, operations automation. If the answer to your revenue problem is a thing that does not exist yet, I would rather build it than write you a specification and hand it to someone else. The same applies to marketing: I will take the whole strategy, not just the part that fits in a slide.

    How do I get more clients?

    Usually the constraint is not lead volume but conversion and clarity. Before spending more on acquisition, check three things: whether your positioning is specific enough to exclude the wrong buyers, whether the people who do sign up reach first value in week one, and whether your best segment is identifiable from your closed-won data. Most businesses asking for more leads already have enough traffic and are losing it between the landing page and the first successful use of the product.

    How do you improve activation?

    Identify the single action that delivers first value, measure what share of signups reach it within a week, then remove everything between signup and that moment. Pre-populating new accounts with realistic sample data is the most reliable single change I know. The long version is here.

    When should a business change its pricing?

    If pricing has never been revisited since launch, it is almost certainly wrong. Start with the billing metric rather than the number: if a customer succeeds wildly with what you sell and their invoice does not grow, the model caps your revenue. A 10–15% increase applied only to new customers is the lowest-risk experiment available. The long version is here.

    How much does an engagement cost?

    Three formats: a 90-minute teardown from €500, a two-week embedded sprint from €6,000, and ongoing fractional work from €8,000 per month with a three-month minimum. Hybrid and on-site delivery cost more than remote. Full pricing is above.
