Why your activation is flat, and what actually fixes it

Activation 5 min read Quentin Dupard Updated August 2026

Flat activation is almost never an onboarding problem, which is unfortunate, because onboarding is what most teams build when activation is flat.

Tours, checklists, welcome emails, progress bars. All of it is decoration applied to a distance that is simply too long. If it takes eleven steps and a colleague's help before anything useful happens, a nicer tour of those eleven steps will not save you.

Find the moment

Every product has a point where a new user goes from sceptical to convinced. Not signup. Not completing setup. The specific instant where the thing does what it promised and they can see it.

For an analytics product it is seeing their own data in a chart, not sample data. For a scheduling tool it is someone else booking a slot. For a writing tool it is finishing something they would have written anyway, faster.

Name it precisely. Vague definitions produce vague metrics, and you will end up optimising "completed onboarding", which measures compliance with your flow rather than value received.

Measure it honestly

What percentage of signups reach that moment within seven days?

Almost nobody has this number when I ask. When they go and get it, the answer is usually far lower than anyone in the room guessed. That figure is the ceiling on everything your marketing spend can achieve. Doubling traffic against a low activation rate just doubles the number of people who form a negative first impression.

Activation is a multiplier on every acquisition euro you will ever spend. Fix it before you spend more, not after.

Cut the distance

Go through the path from signup to that moment and be ruthless about three categories:

  • Fields you do not need yet. Company size, role, phone number, team name. Every one is a chance to abandon, collected for a sales process that has not started. Ask later.
  • Empty states. A blank dashboard is the most common activation killer in software. Someone signed up curious and you showed them a void and asked them to fill it.
  • Dependencies on other people. Anything requiring an admin, an integration, an IT ticket, or a colleague to accept an invite adds days, and days are where intent dies.

The change that works most often

Pre-populate new accounts with realistic sample data so the product is never empty on first open. This is also the cheapest way to find out whether people even want the thing, before you go and argue about what to charge for it.

Not a tour explaining what would be there. Actual data, in the real interface, that the user can click, filter, sort and break. It lets someone experience the product working before they have invested anything, which inverts the usual order where you demand investment before demonstrating value.

Show the product working before you ask anyone to make it work.

This is more effort than a checklist, which is why teams reach for the checklist. It is also, in my experience, the single change most likely to move the number.

What to do on Monday

  1. Write down your activation moment in one sentence. Get three colleagues to agree it is the right one.
  2. Query what share of last quarter's signups reached it inside seven days.
  3. Sit down and complete signup yourself, on a phone, on the real product, timing each step.
  4. Delete the worst step. Then measure again in a fortnight.

Common questions

What is activation in SaaS?

The point at which a new user reaches the moment the product visibly works for them. Not signup, not completing onboarding: the specific action that makes someone think the product does what it promised.

How do I measure activation?

Name the first-value action precisely, then measure what share of signups reach it within seven days. Most teams have never calculated this and find the number far lower than they expected.

How do I improve activation quickly?

Pre-populate new accounts with realistic sample data so the product is never empty on first open, and remove any signup field, empty state or third-party dependency standing between signup and first value.
