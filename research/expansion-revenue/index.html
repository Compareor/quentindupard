Nobody churns, nobody spends more: fixing flat expansion revenue

Retention 6 min read Quentin Dupard Updated August 2026

This is the most flattering-looking problem in software. Churn is low, NPS is fine, customers say nice things on calls, and revenue is flat. Everything looks healthy right up until you model next year.

Retention and expansion are different mechanics and they fail for different reasons. Retention means the product is worth keeping. Expansion means a customer's own success costs them more money. You can have the first without the second, and if you do, you have built a business with a hard revenue ceiling per account.

Why the number matters more than it looks

Net revenue retention is expansion minus contraction minus churn, across your existing base only, ignoring new logos. Above roughly 110%, existing customers alone grow the business faster than churn shrinks it. That is what compounding means in practice.

Below 100%, every quarter starts in a hole. Sales is not growing the business, it is refilling it. That is survivable while acquisition is cheap and fatal when it isn't, which is why this metric tends to become urgent at exactly the wrong moment.

Retention keeps the business alive. Expansion is what makes it worth owning.

Three questions that find the cause

1. Is there an axis along which success costs more?

Seats, usage, volume, transactions, workflows, storage, locations. Something. When a customer doubles the value they get from you, some number on their invoice has to move. If nothing does, no playbook fixes it.

This is where per-seat pricing quietly betrays you. It only tracks value when value genuinely scales with headcount. A customer processing ten times the data with the same team is getting ten times the value at the same price.

2. Can a power user outgrow your top tier?

Many products have a ceiling everyone hits in month two. Once a customer is on the highest plan with every feature unlocked, they are done. There is nowhere left for them to go, and you have capped yourself.

The fix is not always a more expensive tier. Sometimes it is an add-on, a second product, or a usage component layered onto a flat platform fee. But something has to exist above where your best customers currently sit.

3. Do you find out at renewal?

If the first time anyone notices an account has outgrown its plan is the renewal conversation, you have been giving away the upgrade for months. Instrument the moment a customer crosses a threshold, and make the upgrade path something they can take themselves.

If a customer has to book a call with a human to give you more money, you will collect less of it.

What I would do first

  1. Calculate net revenue retention on the existing base only. Exclude new logos entirely or the number lies to you.
  2. Split it by segment. A healthy blended figure often hides one segment expanding well and another quietly contracting.
  3. Take your ten happiest customers and ask what would have to be true for them to pay you double. If the answer is "nothing we sell", that is your roadmap.
  4. Make the most common upgrade self-serve before you build anything new.

Most teams reach for customer success here, because expansion feels like a relationship problem. It is usually a packaging problem wearing a relationship costume.

Common questions

What is a good net revenue retention rate?

Above roughly 110% is what makes a subscription business compound, because existing customers alone grow revenue faster than churn removes it. Below 100% you are refilling a leaking bucket with new logos and growth gets more expensive every quarter.

Why is my retention good but revenue flat?

Because retention and expansion are different mechanics. Retention means the product is worth keeping. Expansion means the customer's success costs them more. If there is no axis along which a thriving customer consumes more, you have built a business with a revenue ceiling per account.

Is expansion revenue a customer success problem?

Almost never. It is a packaging problem. No amount of quarterly business reviews will create expansion if your pricing has no mechanism for a growing customer to pay more.
